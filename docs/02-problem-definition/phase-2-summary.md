# BRONNIE — Phase 2 Problem Definition Summary

**Project:** BRONNIE  
**Phase:** Phase 2 — Problem Definition  
**Document Status:** Final  
**Owner:** Technical Lead / Forward Deployed Engineer

---

## 1. Phase Objective

Phase 2 converted discovery evidence into formal business problems and established the problem boundary for the first BRONNIE proof of concept.

---

## 2. Problems Defined

Nine major business problems were identified:

- PS-001 — Manual customer enquiry processing;
- PS-002 — Limited workflow visibility;
- PS-003 — Repetitive appointment coordination;
- PS-004 — Repetitive cross-system data transfer;
- PS-005 — Fragmented customer interaction history;
- PS-006 — Manual supplier invoice processing;
- PS-007 — Manual customer invoice administration;
- PS-008 — Limited operational performance visibility;
- PS-009 — Human-dependent operational scaling.

---

## 3. Root Causes

The primary underlying causes were identified as:

- fragmented business systems;
- manual interpretation;
- manual workflow routing;
- manual data transfer;
- limited workflow state tracking;
- fragmented communication channels;
- limited operational instrumentation;
- human-dependent workflow execution.

---

## 4. Business Impact

These conditions contribute to:

- repetitive administrative effort;
- customer response delays;
- duplicated work;
- data-entry risk;
- booking friction;
- limited management visibility;
- increasing operational cost;
- reduced scalability.

---

## 5. Prioritisation Result

The strongest connected problem cluster for the first BRONNIE POC is:

Customer enquiry  
→ Interpretation  
→ Routing  
→ Booking where relevant  
→ System interaction  
→ Workflow tracking  
→ Completion visibility

This cluster provides high transaction frequency, meaningful business impact and a comparatively controlled environment for demonstrating automation.

---

## 6. Deferred Problems

The following remain potential future BRONNIE capabilities:

- supplier invoice automation;
- customer invoicing;
- telephone/voice automation;
- cross-channel expansion;
- online booking payments.

They are not part of the initial POC.

---

## 7. Target Outcomes

The initial BRONNIE POC should aim to:

- reduce manual enquiry processing;
- improve response times;
- reduce booking coordination effort;
- reduce duplicate data entry;
- improve workflow visibility;
- improve operational measurement;
- maintain appropriate human oversight;
- demonstrate measurable business value.

---

## 8. POC Problem Boundary

The approved initial problem boundary is:

**A customer-operations workflow orchestration POC demonstrating enquiry interpretation, routing, appointment coordination, controlled system interaction, workflow tracking and operational visibility.**

---

## 9. Architecture Status

No final architecture has been selected during Phase 2.

Technology directions already identified remain hypotheses or project constraints until later phases validate them.

The project must not begin implementation based solely on the Phase 2 problem definition.

---

## 10. Phase 2 Exit Criteria

Phase 2 is complete when:

- problem statements are documented;
- root causes are documented;
- business impacts are understood;
- problems are prioritised;
- target outcomes are defined;
- the initial POC problem boundary is documented;
- the documents have been reviewed and committed.

---

## 11. Phase 2 Exit Decision

**Phase 2 Status: COMPLETE**

**Next Phase: Phase 3 — Current-State Analysis**

Phase 3 will deepen the preliminary workflow information gathered during discovery by formally analysing:

- detailed process flows;
- actors and responsibilities;
- system hand-offs;
- data movement;
- decision points;
- exceptions;
- bottlenecks;
- workflow baselines;
- control points.

Phase 3 must not be skipped simply because preliminary current workflows were captured during discovery.

Discovery described the workflows.

Phase 2 identified the problems.

Phase 3 will formally analyse how the selected POC workflows currently operate before requirements are written.
