# BRONNIE — Problem Statements

**Project:** BRONNIE  
**Phase:** Phase 2 — Problem Definition  
**Document Status:** Complete  
**Owner:** Technical Lead / Forward Deployed Engineer

---

## 1. Purpose

This document converts Phase 1 discovery evidence into formal business problem statements.

The statements describe business problems without prescribing technical solutions.

---

## 2. Problem Definition Principles

BRONNIE problem statements must:

- be supported by discovery evidence;
- describe business problems rather than technologies;
- distinguish symptoms from underlying causes;
- identify affected stakeholders;
- identify business impact;
- eventually support measurable outcomes.

---

## 3. PS-001 — Customer Enquiry Processing Requires Significant Manual Effort

### Problem Statement

The organisation receives a high volume of customer communications that require administrative employees to manually read, interpret, classify, route and respond to requests.

### Evidence

Discovery estimated:

- 180–250 incoming emails per business day;
- 50–80 telephone calls per business day.

Administrative employees manually determine the intent, urgency, appropriate recipient, required action and follow-up.

### Affected Stakeholders

- Administration / Reception
- Customers
- Sales
- Operations
- Management

### Business Impact

- repetitive administrative work;
- slower customer responses;
- inconsistent processing;
- risk of missed requests;
- reduced capacity for higher-value work.

---

## 4. PS-002 — Customer Requests Lack End-to-End Workflow Visibility

### Problem Statement

When requests move between employees and systems, the organisation has limited visibility into ownership, current status and completion.

### Evidence

Requests may be forwarded, converted into tasks, entered into another system or handled through another channel.

Visibility decreases after these hand-offs.

### Affected Stakeholders

- Administration
- Operations
- Customers
- Sales
- Management

### Business Impact

- unclear ownership;
- missed follow-up;
- duplicated work;
- delayed responses;
- difficulty measuring completion.

---

## 5. PS-003 — Appointment Coordination Requires Repetitive Administrative Work

### Problem Statement

Appointment booking, rescheduling and cancellation require administrative employees to repeatedly coordinate customer requirements with employee availability.

### Evidence

Discovery estimated approximately 150–200 bookings or booking changes per week.

Straightforward bookings may require approximately 5–10 minutes of administrative effort, with complex bookings requiring longer.

### Affected Stakeholders

- Administration / Reception
- Customers
- Service Employees
- Operations

### Business Impact

- repetitive calendar checking;
- repeated customer communication;
- administrative workload;
- booking delays;
- scheduling friction.

---

## 6. PS-004 — Information Is Repeatedly Transferred Between Systems

### Problem Statement

Employees frequently receive information in one system and manually enter the same information into another system to continue a workflow.

### Evidence

A recurring pattern identified during discovery was:

Information received  
→ Human interpretation  
→ Workflow selected  
→ Another system opened  
→ Information manually transferred  
→ Action performed

### Affected Stakeholders

- Administration
- Operations
- Finance
- Sales
- IT / Systems

### Business Impact

- duplicated effort;
- processing delays;
- data-entry errors;
- inconsistent records;
- reduced scalability.

---

## 7. PS-005 — Customer Interaction History Is Fragmented Across Channels

### Problem Statement

Customer interactions occur across email and telephone without a consistently connected view of previous communication and workflow status.

### Evidence

Customers may email and later call regarding the same unresolved request.

The employee answering the telephone may not immediately know what has already occurred.

### Business Impact

- customers repeat information;
- duplicated work;
- slower service;
- inconsistent responses;
- fragmented customer experience.

---

## 8. PS-006 — Supplier Invoice Processing Contains Significant Manual Work

### Problem Statement

Supplier invoices require employees to manually open documents, interpret information, validate details and transfer information into accounting processes.

### Evidence

Approximately 250–350 supplier invoices per month were estimated during discovery.

### Business Impact

- repetitive document processing;
- manual data extraction;
- manual data entry;
- processing delays;
- financial processing risk.

Financial approval and payment authority must remain appropriately controlled.

---

## 9. PS-007 — Customer Invoice Administration Requires Repetitive Manual Work

### Problem Statement

Customer invoice preparation and follow-up require employees to gather billing information, verify details, issue invoices and monitor payment.

### Evidence

Approximately 400 customer invoices per month were estimated during discovery.

### Business Impact

- administrative workload;
- potential invoicing delays;
- repetitive follow-up;
- billing error risk.

---

## 10. PS-008 — Management Lacks Reliable Operational Visibility

### Problem Statement

Management lacks sufficient quantitative visibility into administrative workload, workflow performance and operational failures.

### Missing Measurements

Examples include:

- customer response time;
- processing time;
- missed-request rate;
- booking coordination time;
- error and rework rates;
- cost per transaction;
- workload by workflow.

### Business Impact

Management has difficulty determining where operational improvements will produce the greatest value and whether changes actually improve performance.

---

## 11. PS-009 — Administrative Workload Scales With Business Growth

### Problem Statement

Many workflows require human effort at multiple stages, causing administrative workload to increase as transaction volume grows.

### Evidence

Management reported that additional administrative staffing has not eliminated workload pressure.

### Business Impact

- increasing operating cost;
- increasing staffing requirements;
- limited scalability;
- workload pressure;
- potential service degradation during growth.

---

## 12. Relationship Between Problems

The problems are interconnected.

High transaction volume  
→ Manual interpretation  
→ Manual routing  
→ Manual system switching  
→ Manual data transfer  
→ Workflow hand-offs  
→ Limited visibility  
→ Delays / duplicated work / missed requests  
→ Increased administrative workload

The next analysis determines which underlying causes produce these problems.
