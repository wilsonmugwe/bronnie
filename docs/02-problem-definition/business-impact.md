# BRONNIE — Business Impact Analysis

**Project:** BRONNIE  
**Phase:** Phase 2 — Problem Definition  
**Document Status:** Complete  
**Owner:** Technical Lead / Forward Deployed Engineer

---

## 1. Purpose

This document describes how the identified operational problems affect the organisation.

---

## 2. Employee Productivity Impact

Administrative employees spend significant time on:

- reading communications;
- classifying requests;
- routing work;
- checking calendars;
- transferring information;
- processing documents;
- preparing invoices;
- performing follow-up.

This reduces time available for activities requiring greater human judgement or customer attention.

---

## 3. Customer Experience Impact

Operational fragmentation can contribute to:

- slower responses;
- repeated requests for information;
- booking delays;
- inconsistent communication;
- customers following up through additional channels.

Customers have reportedly followed up by telephone after waiting for email responses.

---

## 4. Operational Cost Impact

When transaction volume increases, manual processing requirements also increase.

The current operating model may therefore require additional administrative capacity as the business grows.

This creates a potentially increasing cost-to-serve.

Exact financial impact has not yet been quantified.

---

## 5. Scalability Impact

Current workflows depend heavily on employees acting as coordinators between systems.

This means increased business volume can create:

- more emails;
- more calls;
- more bookings;
- more documents;
- more invoices;
- more manual data transfer.

Without process improvement, operational capacity may remain strongly linked to administrative headcount.

---

## 6. Data Quality Impact

Manual data transfer introduces potential:

- typing errors;
- missing information;
- inconsistent records;
- duplicated information;
- rework.

Exact error rates remain unmeasured.

---

## 7. Management Impact

Limited operational measurement makes it difficult for management to:

- identify bottlenecks;
- determine staffing requirements;
- prioritise improvements;
- identify workflow failures;
- calculate operational cost;
- measure ROI from automation.

---

## 8. Financial Administration Impact

Supplier and customer invoice processes contain repetitive work.

Potential impacts include:

- processing delays;
- delayed invoice issuance;
- delayed payment;
- data-entry errors;
- additional follow-up effort.

Financial workflows also introduce higher risk and stronger approval requirements.

---

## 9. Risk Impact

Automation itself can introduce additional risks if poorly controlled.

These include:

- incorrect AI interpretation;
- unauthorised actions;
- sensitive data exposure;
- incorrect financial actions;
- integration failures.

Therefore business value must be balanced against operational and security risk.

---

## 10. Overall Business Impact

The combined effect of the identified problems is:

Increasing business activity  
→ Increasing manual workload  
→ Higher administrative demand  
→ Potential response delays  
→ Reduced operational visibility  
→ Increasing cost and scalability pressure

The strongest BRONNIE opportunities should therefore reduce repetitive human effort while improving reliability and visibility.
