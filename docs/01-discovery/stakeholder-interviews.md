# BRONNIE — Stakeholder Interview Findings

**Project:** BRONNIE  
**Phase:** Phase 1 — Discovery  
**Status:** Complete

---

## 1. Purpose

This document consolidates findings from stakeholder discussions conducted
during discovery.

Stakeholder statements are treated as discovery evidence rather than
formal system requirements.

---

## 2. Managing Director

### Primary Concerns

The Managing Director reported that:

- administrative employees are consistently busy;
- significant time is spent on repetitive work;
- customer requests can be delayed;
- some requests are occasionally missed;
- information is manually copied between systems;
- appointment coordination consumes administrative time;
- document processing requires manual effort;
- management lacks visibility into where administrative time is spent.

Additional administrative staff have previously been added, but workload
pressure has continued to increase.

### Business Objective

The objective is not simply to replace administrative staff.

Management wants employees to spend less time on repetitive administration
and more time on higher-value work.

---

## 3. Operations Management

Operational workflows involve significant movement of information between:

- customer communications;
- administrative staff;
- calendars;
- customer records;
- documents;
- accounting processes;
- internal employees.

Operational concerns include:

- repetitive processing;
- manual hand-offs;
- inconsistent workflow visibility;
- difficulty tracking completion;
- limited performance measurement;
- workload increasing alongside business growth.

---

## 4. Administration / Reception

Administration handles significant portions of:

- shared email;
- telephone calls;
- customer enquiries;
- appointment booking;
- appointment changes;
- appointment cancellations;
- customer information entry;
- internal routing;
- follow-up.

### Email

Employees read incoming emails and determine:

- what the customer wants;
- whether they can answer directly;
- whether another employee should handle the request;
- whether information needs to be entered into another system.

### Telephone

Customers also contact the organisation by telephone.

Calls may involve:

- new enquiries;
- bookings;
- rescheduling;
- cancellations;
- service questions;
- follow-up on previous communications;
- requests to speak with employees.

Call information is not always captured consistently.

### Cross-Channel Issue

Customers may email and later telephone about the same matter.

The employee answering the telephone may not immediately have complete
visibility into the earlier email interaction.

---

## 5. Accounts / Finance

Discovery identified two distinct invoice workflows.

### Supplier Invoices

Supplier invoices commonly arrive by email as PDF attachments.

Staff may:

1. open the email;
2. download/open the invoice;
3. read the document;
4. identify the supplier;
5. identify relevant invoice information;
6. validate the invoice;
7. enter information into the accounting process;
8. route the invoice for approval where required;
9. continue into the payment process.

Financial approval remains a controlled business activity.

### Customer Invoices

After work is completed:

1. billing information is gathered;
2. an invoice is prepared;
3. customer details are checked;
4. invoice amounts are checked;
5. the invoice is issued;
6. payment status is monitored;
7. overdue payments may require follow-up.

Supplier invoice processing and customer invoicing are therefore separate
business workflows.

---

## 6. Sales

Customer enquiries may arrive through email or telephone.

Enquiries can require:

- classification;
- customer information capture;
- routing;
- internal follow-up;
- response;
- possible appointment creation.

Slow routing may affect customer response time and potential sales
opportunities.

---

## 7. IT / Systems

Multiple system categories appear to participate in the current workflows:

- email;
- calendars;
- customer/CRM information;
- accounting;
- documents;
- telephone;
- internal task/communication systems.

Employees currently perform significant manual integration between these
systems.

Exact products, APIs, access methods and integration capabilities remain
subject to technical verification.

---

## 8. Cross-Stakeholder Themes

Recurring themes include:

- repetitive administrative work;
- high communication volume;
- manual interpretation;
- manual data entry;
- fragmented systems;
- repeated hand-offs;
- inconsistent cross-channel visibility;
- limited operational measurement;
- growing workload;
- customer response delays;
- requirement for human oversight of sensitive actions.

---

## 9. Discovery Conclusion

The stakeholder discussions indicate that the underlying challenge is
broader than a single email, booking or invoice problem.

The organisation experiences operational fragmentation across multiple
business processes.

These findings will be converted into formal problem statements during
Phase 2.
